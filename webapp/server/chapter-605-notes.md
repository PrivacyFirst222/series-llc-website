# Chapter 605 — reading notes

Verified against the statutory text on Online Sunshine (leg.state.fl.us), read
through the browser rather than a summarizing fetch tool. The whole chapter is
417,137 characters; the protected series provisions (ss. 605.2101–605.2802) are
75,474 of them.

**Why this file exists.** A summarizer cannot tell you that you are wrong,
because what matters is usually the clause it dropped. Findings recorded here
are quoted or closely paraphrased from text actually read, so they can be
checked against the statute rather than against my memory of it.

**Effective date.** Ch. 2025-162 added ss. 605.2101–605.2802, effective
July 1, 2026. s. 605.2802(2): a domestic LLC formed before July 1, 2026 could
not create or designate a protected series before that date.

## CORRECTION — s. 605.2107 (non-variable provisions)

I previously asserted that s. 605.2107 does not make s. 605.2303 non-variable.
That is wrong. s. 605.2107(1) provides "An operating agreement may not vary the
effect of," and lists at (m) **"Section 605.2303(1) or (2)."**

Full list: (a) this section; (b) 605.2103; (c) 605.2104(1); (d) 605.2104(2), to
provide a protected series a power beyond those the chapter provides an LLC;
(e) 605.2104(3) or (4); (f) 605.2105; (g) 605.2106; (h) 605.2108; (i) 605.2201,
except the manner of approving establishment; (j) 605.2202; (k) 605.2301;
(l) 605.2302; (m) 605.2303(1) or (2); (n) 605.2304(3) or (6); (o) 605.2401,
except to decrease or eliminate a limitation of liability; (p) 605.2402;
(q) 605.2403; (r) 605.2404; (s) 605.2501(1), (4), (5); (t) 605.2502, except to
designate a different person to manage winding up; (u) 605.2503;
(v) 605.2601–605.2608; (w) 605.2701–605.2704; (x) 605.2801–605.2802, except who
may sign and deliver records; (y) provisions on registered offices/agents and on
the department.

s. 605.2107(2): an operating agreement may not unreasonably restrict the duties
and rights conferred under s. 605.2305, but may impose reasonable restrictions
on availability and use of information and provide remedies including liquidated
damages.

**Effect on our masters: none.** New s. 3.6(c) rests on s. 605.2303(4) and
s. 14.1 on s. 605.2501(2) — neither is in the non-variable list.

## Company-owned series — statutory support

- **s. 605.2303(1)** — a protected-series transferable interest "must be owned
  initially by an associated member of the protected series or the series
  limited liability company."
- **s. 605.2303(2)** — "If a protected series of a series limited liability
  company has no associated members when established, the company owns the
  protected-series transferable interests in the protected series."
- **s. 605.2303(3)** — the company may also acquire an interest by transfer or
  as the operating agreement provides.
- **s. 605.2303(4)** — chapter and operating-agreement provisions applying to a
  protected-series transferee apply to the company as owner, except
  s. 605.2108(1)(c).
- **s. 605.2302(1)** — "Only a member of a series limited liability company may
  be an associated member of a protected series of the company." The company
  cannot be one.
- **s. 605.2304(2)** — "If a protected series has no associated members, the
  series limited liability company is the protected-series manager." Not in the
  non-variable list, so the operating agreement may designate otherwise.
- **s. 605.2401(2)(c)3** — the company is NOT liable for a protected series'
  debts solely by reason of "owning a protected-series transferable interest of
  the protected series." Answers the obvious objection to company ownership.
- **s. 605.2105(4)(d), (5)(b)** — same point in the governing-law section.
- **s. 605.2403(2)** — s. 605.0503's charging-order restrictions apply to a
  judgment creditor of "a series limited liability company, to the extent the
  company owns a protected-series transferable interest of a protected series."
- **s. 605.2501** — no dissolution event for lacking associated members.
  (1) dissolution of the company; (2) an event the operating agreement states;
  (3) consent of all associated members; (4)–(5) judicial; (6) automatic or
  involuntary dissolution of the company; (7) administrative dissolution.
- **Definitional tension worth knowing:** s. 605.2102(10) defines a
  protected-series transferable interest as the right "as initially owned by a
  person in the person's capacity as an associated member." s. 605.2303(2) and
  s. 605.2105(4)(d)/(5)(b) and s. 605.2403(2) nevertheless expressly contemplate
  company ownership, so the structure holds.

## Shields, formalities, burden of proof

- **s. 605.2401(2)** — a debt of the company is solely the company's; a debt of
  a protected series is solely that series'; neither is liable for the other's
  solely by reason of the relationships listed.
- **s. 605.2402(2)** — "The failure of a limited liability company or a
  protected series to observe formalities relating to the exercise of its powers
  or management of its activities and affairs is **not** a ground to disregard a
  limitation in s. 605.2401(1) but **may** be a ground to disregard a limitation
  in s. 605.2401(2)." Sloppy formalities cannot reach the members personally but
  can collapse the shields between company and series. NOT currently on the site.
- **s. 605.2402(1)** — piercing claims are governed by law and equity as if each
  protected series were a separately formed LLC.
- **s. 605.2404(4)** — "the party asserting that an asset is or was an
  associated asset ... has the burden of proof on the issue." The owner carries
  it. NOT currently on the site.
- **s. 605.2404(2)** — a judgment against the company may be enforced against a
  series' asset that was or is a NON-associated asset on the incurrence or
  enforcement date, and vice versa, and between series.
- **s. 605.2301(2)(a)** — records must let a disinterested, reasonable
  individual identify the asset, determine when and from whom acquired, and, if
  acquired from the company or another series, the consideration, payor, payee.
- **s. 605.2301(4)** — records may be organized by listing, category, type,
  quantity, or computational or allocative formula, "or in any other reasonable
  manner."
- **s. 605.2301(5)** — a protected series may not hold an associated asset in
  the name of the company or another series, and vice versa.
- **s. 605.2301(2)(b)** — a recorded deed or instrument in favor of a person who
  gives value without knowledge of a lack of authority "is conclusive of the
  authority of the person signing and constitutes a record that such interest in
  real property is an associated asset or liability."

## Administration

- **s. 605.2203(1)** — "The registered agent in this state for a series limited
  liability company is the registered agent in this state for each protected
  series of that company." One agent covers everything. Supports charging no
  separate registered agent designation fee.
- **s. 605.2203(4)–(5)** — ceasing to be agent for the company ends the agency
  for every series, and vice versa.
- **s. 605.2206(1)–(2)** — the annual report under s. 605.0212 must name each
  protected series; failure "prevents issuance of a certificate of status
  pertaining to the protected series, but does not otherwise affect the
  protected series." No per-series fee appears in the statute — that is a
  Department fee-schedule question.
- **s. 605.2202(2)** — the name must begin with the company's name and contain
  "protected series" or the abbreviation "P.S." or "PS." No dash is required by
  statute; the dash is how Sunbiz renders it.
- **s. 605.2201(1)–(2)** — unanimous member vote or consent to establish; the
  designation states the company name and the series name.
- **s. 605.2104(2)–(4)** — a series has the same powers and purposes as the
  company EXCEPT that it may not be a member of the company, may not establish a
  protected series, and ceases to exist when the company completes winding up.
- **s. 605.2602–605.2604** — a protected series may not convert, domesticate, or
  participate in an interest exchange, and may merge only through the single
  channel in s. 605.2604 (every other party an LLC; surviving company not
  created in the merger).
- **s. 605.2108(1)** — the deeming rule applies in the application of
  ss. 605.2106, 605.2304(3) and (6), 605.2501(4)(a), 605.2502(1), and
  605.2503(2). (Our old statute page labeled 605.2503(2) a "claims process"; it
  is reinstatement. Section since removed.)

## General act — ss. 605.0101–605.0108 (read)

- **s. 605.0102(37) "Majority-in-interest"** is defined by PROFITS: members
  holding "more than 50 percent of the then-current percentage or other interest
  in the profits of the limited liability company owned by all of its members."
  Our masters define "Majority in Interest" by Percentage Interests. The two
  coincide in our forms because allocations and distributions are strictly pro
  rata to Percentage Interests, but the defined terms are not worded the same.
- **s. 605.0102(45)** — an operating agreement "may be oral, implied, in a
  record, or in any combination thereof." Our s. 15.1 says oral, implied, and
  course-of-dealing amendments are of no effect; s. 605.0105(1)(d) lets the
  agreement govern "the means and conditions for amending the operating
  agreement," so that restriction is permitted.
- **s. 605.0105(3)** — what an operating agreement may NOT do. Relevant limits:
  (c) registered agents and department filings; (e)–(f) may not eliminate the
  duty of loyalty or care, or the obligation of good faith and fair dealing;
  (g) may not exonerate bad faith, willful or intentional misconduct, or a
  knowing violation of law; (h) may not unreasonably restrict information rights
  under s. 605.0410; (i) may not vary the grounds for dissolution in
  s. 605.0702 — but expressly, **"A deadlock resolution mechanism does not vary
  the grounds for dissolution for the purposes of this paragraph"** (blesses our
  Article 13 deadlock/buy-sell); (k) may not unreasonably restrict a member's
  right to sue under ss. 605.0801–605.0806; (p) may not indemnify for bad faith,
  willful or intentional misconduct, improper personal benefit, or s. 605.0406
  liability.
- **s. 605.0105(4)(c)** — if not manifestly unreasonable, the agreement may
  alter or eliminate aspects of the duty of loyalty, identify categories of
  activity that do not violate it, alter the duty of care (but not authorize
  willful misconduct), and alter or eliminate any other fiduciary duty. Supports
  the Article 4 competition alternatives.
- **s. 605.0105(5)** — "manifestly unreasonable" is decided by the court as a
  matter of law, judged as of when the term entered the agreement.
- **s. 605.0106(5)** — a one-member operating agreement "is not unenforceable
  simply because there is only one person who is a party." Supports the
  single-member master.
- **s. 605.0106(6)** — an operating agreement is not subject to the statute of
  frauds except as provided in s. 605.0403(1), which is why our s. 6.1 requires
  a signed writing for a promised contribution.
- **s. 605.0107(4)** — where a filed record conflicts with the operating
  agreement, the agreement prevails as to members, dissociated members,
  transferees and managers; the record prevails as to others who reasonably rely
  on it.
- **s. 605.0103(4)** — a non-member is deemed to know of a limitation on
  authority to transfer real property under s. 605.0302(7), and to have notice
  of dissolution, termination, entity transactions, and a manager-managed
  declaration 90 days after the relevant filing takes effect. A limitation in
  the articles on authority to transfer real property is NOT notice to a
  non-member unless recorded in the real property records.
- **s. 605.0108(1)** — "A limited liability company is an entity distinct from
  its members."

## ⚠ OPEN QUESTION — may a protected series serve as a registered agent?

**Why this matters:** our registered agent of record is "FLORIDA PROTECTED
SERIES, LLC - PS 2" — a protected series, not the company.

**s. 605.0113(1)(b)** — a registered agent must be:
1. "An individual who resides in this state and whose business address is
   identical to the address of the registered office;
2. Another domestic entity that is an authorized entity and whose business
   address is identical to the address of the registered office; or
3. A foreign entity authorized to transact business in this state that is an
   authorized entity..."

**s. 605.0113(6)** — "For the purposes of this section, 'authorized entity'
means: (a) A corporation for profit. (b) A limited liability company. (c) A
limited liability partnership. (d) A limited partnership, including a limited
liability limited partnership."

A protected series is not named in that list. The question is whether it
qualifies as "a limited liability company" or as a "domestic entity."

**Arguments it qualifies:**
- s. 605.2108(3) — "the provisions of this chapter applicable to limited
  liability companies in general ... including, but not limited to, provisions
  relating to formation, powers, operation, existence, management, court
  proceedings, and filings with the department ... are applicable to each series
  limited liability company and to each protected series established pursuant to
  s. 605.2201."
- s. 605.2104(2) — a protected series "has the same powers and purposes as the
  series limited liability company."
- s. 605.2102(7) — a protected series is a "person."

**Arguments it does not:**
- s. 605.0113(6) enumerates entity TYPES; a protected series is not one, and
  s. 605.2103 makes it a person distinct from the company rather than an LLC.
- s. 605.2108(1) deems a series to BE a separately formed LLC only for
  ss. 605.2106, 605.2304(3) and (6), 605.2501(4)(a), 605.2502(1), and
  605.2503(2). s. 605.0113 is not among them.
- s. 605.0102(23) defines "entity" by a list that does not include a protected
  series; the catch-all is "any other domestic or foreign entity that is
  organized under an organic law."
- s. 605.2104(4) already shows the Act states expressly when a series may not do
  something; it is silent on serving as another person's registered agent.

**Unresolved.** Needs Adam's judgment. If the answer is no, every registered
agent designation naming PS 2 may be defective, and the fix is to name the
company (FLORIDA PROTECTED SERIES, LLC) instead.

## Findings — ss. 605.0109-605.0216

- **s. 605.0110(4)** — a member "has no interest in any specific limited
  liability company property." Basis for our s. 6.5.
- **s. 605.0111(1)** — the chapter intends "maximum effect to the principle of
  freedom of contract and to the enforceability of operating agreements."
- **s. 605.01125** — name reservation exists, nonrenewable 120 days. We do not
  offer it; it is not unavailable.
- **s. 605.0112(1)(b)** — names must be distinguishable; a suffix, article,
  "and"/"&", singular/plural/possessive, or punctuation does NOT distinguish.
- **s. 605.0201(2)** — articles state the name, street AND mailing address of the
  principal office, and the name, Florida street address, and written acceptance
  of the initial registered agent.
- **s. 605.0201(4)** — formed when the articles take effect AND at least one
  person becomes a member at that moment.
- **s. 605.0203(1)(b)** — initial articles signed by an authorized
  representative, with the agent's signed acceptance included or attached.
- **s. 605.0205(3)** — a signer "affirms under penalty of perjury that the
  information stated in the record is accurate."
- **s. 605.0207** — initial articles may be backdated up to 5 business days; a
  delayed effective date is capped at the 90th day after filing.
- **s. 605.0209** — statement of correction for a record that was inaccurate,
  defectively signed, or contains false information; retroactive except as to
  those who relied; no fee within 15 days of the filing notice for false,
  misleading or fraudulent information. This is the cure if the registered agent
  question goes against PS 2.
- **s. 605.0210(5)** — the department's duty to file is **ministerial**; filing
  creates no presumption that the contents are correct.
- **s. 605.0211(3)** — a certificate of status is conclusive evidence of
  existence and active status.
- **s. 605.0212(1)(d)** — the annual report must state the FEIN "or, if none,
  whether one has been applied for."
- **s. 605.0212(6)** — a company that has not filed its annual report "may not
  maintain or defend any action in a court of this state" until it is filed and
  all fees and penalties are paid.
- **s. 605.0117(3)** — the subsection on serving a "registered series of a
  foreign series limited liability company" was DELETED effective July 1, 2026.

## Findings — ss. 605.0301-605.0704

**Authority and real property**
- **s. 605.04074(3)** — "Unless a certified statement of authority recorded in
  the applicable real estate records limits the authority of a member or a
  manager," a member of a member-managed company (or manager of a
  manager-managed one) "may sign and deliver an instrument transferring or
  affecting the limited liability company's interest in real property. The
  instrument is conclusive in favor of a person who gives value without
  knowledge of the lack of the authority." So an internal restriction in the
  operating agreement does NOT protect against a rogue conveyance to a
  good-faith purchaser — only a RECORDED certified statement of authority does.
  This is precisely why our member-managed masters carry s. 5.9.
- **s. 605.0302(7)** — if a certified copy of a statement of authority containing
  a limitation on authority to transfer real property is recorded in the real
  property records, "all persons are deemed to know of the limitation."
- **s. 605.0302(10)** — an effective statement of authority "is canceled by
  operation of law 5 years after" it or its most recent amendment became
  effective.
- **s. 605.0301** — a person binds the company only through s. 605.04074 agency,
  the articles or operating agreement, a filed statement of authority, or other
  law.

**Capital and distributions**
- **s. 605.0403(1)** — "A promise by a person to contribute to the limited
  liability company is not enforceable unless it is set out in a writing signed
  by the person." Basis for our s. 6.1.
- **s. 605.0403(5)** — an operating agreement may impose consequences for failure
  to contribute, expressly including "reducing or eliminating the defaulting
  member's proportionate interest," subordination, forced sale, forfeiture,
  lending by other members, or appraisal/formula redemption. Direct authority for
  our s. 6.3 dilution mechanic.
- **s. 605.0404(1), (5)** — the DEFAULT is that distributions and profits/losses
  are shared "on the basis of the agreed value, as stated in the company's
  records, of the contributions made by each" member — not per capita and not by
  percentage interest. Our masters override this by stating percentages, which
  s. 605.0105(1) permits.
- **s. 605.0405** — no distribution if the company could not then pay its debts as
  they come due, or if total assets would be less than total liabilities plus
  preferential amounts. s. 605.0406 — consenting members/managers personally
  liable for the excess; action barred after 2 years.

**Management, duties, liability**
- **s. 605.0407(1)** — member-managed by default; "managing member" alone does
  NOT make a company manager-managed.
- **s. 605.04072(1), (4)** — a manager is chosen and removed by consent of members
  holding more than 50 percent of the interest in profits.
- **s. 605.04073(1)(c)** — member-managed: majority-in-interest is required to
  undertake an act "whether within or outside the ordinary course."
  **(2)(d)** — manager-managed: majority-in-interest required for acts OUTSIDE
  the ordinary course.
- **s. 605.04073(1)(d), (2)(e)** — the operating agreement and articles "may be
  amended only with the affirmative vote or consent of all members." Matches our
  s. 15.1.
- **s. 605.04091(2)(c)** — the duty of loyalty includes "refraining from competing
  with the company in the conduct of the company's activities and affairs before
  the dissolution of the company." Correctly cited in our Article 4 Alternative A.
- **s. 605.04091(5)** — a member or manager "does not violate a duty or obligation
  ... solely because the ... conduct furthers the ... own interest."
- **s. 605.04093** — no personal liability for monetary damages for management
  decisions unless the breach constitutes a criminal violation, an improper
  personal benefit, a s. 605.0406 distribution, conscious disregard or willful
  misconduct (derivative claims), or recklessness/bad faith/malice (third-party
  claims).
- **s. 605.0408(2)** — indemnification is permitted unless the liability arises
  from breach of ss. 605.0405, .0407, .04071, .04072, .04073, .04074 or .04091.
  Our "to the fullest extent permitted by the Act" tracks this.
- **s. 605.04092(4)(a)** — the conflict-of-interest safe harbor cannot be
  satisfied "solely by a single manager" or "solely by a single member."

**Records**
- **s. 605.0410(1)** — required records include a current list of members and
  managers with addresses; the operating agreement and amendments; the articles
  and other filed documents; **income tax returns and reports for the 3 most
  recent years**; **financial statements for the 3 most recent years**; and a
  record of the agreed value of each member's contributions. Worth confirming
  our Article 8 lists the three-year tax return and financial statement items.
- **s. 605.0410(10)** — the company may impose reasonable confidentiality
  restrictions, but bears the burden of proving reasonableness, and may not
  impose them on the s. 605.0410(1) records.
- **s. 605.0411** — court-ordered inspection at the company's expense, with fees,
  unless the company proves a reasonable basis for doubt.

**Transfers and creditors**
- **s. 605.0502(6)** — a transfer violating an operating-agreement restriction "is
  ineffective as to a person who has knowledge or notice of the restriction."
- **s. 605.0503(3)** — charging order is "the sole and exclusive remedy," except
  as provided in (4) and (5).
- **s. 605.0503(4)** — single-member foreclosure requires the creditor to
  "establish to the satisfaction of a court ... that distributions under a
  charging order will not satisfy the judgment within a reasonable time," and
  then the court "may order the sale of that interest ... pursuant to a
  foreclosure sale." (5) the purchaser takes the entire interest and becomes the
  member.
- **s. 605.0503(6)** — for a multi-member company foreclosure "is not available
  ... and may not be ordered by a court."
- **s. 605.0503(7)** — the section does not limit consensual security interests,
  fraudulent transfer law, or "the equitable principles of alter ego, equitable
  lien, or constructive trust." Charging-order protection is not absolute; the
  site's asset protection page does not mention this.
- **s. 605.0601(1)** — a person "has the power to dissociate as a member at any
  time, rightfully or wrongfully." A no-withdrawal covenant makes withdrawal
  wrongful; it cannot remove the power. Our s. 13.1 is drafted correctly.
- **s. 605.0601(4)** — the articles or operating agreement "may provide that a
  limited liability company interest may not be assigned before the dissolution
  and winding up."

**Dissolution**
- **s. 605.0701(3)** — dissolution after "90 consecutive days during which the
  company has no members," unless transferees owning a majority of distribution
  rights consent to admit a member. Our s. 12.3 addresses this.
- **⭐ s. 605.0702(2) — deadlock sale provisions.** If the operating agreement
  "contains a deadlock sale provision that has been initiated before the time
  that the court determines that the grounds for judicial dissolution exist
  under subparagraph (1)(b)5., then such deadlock sale provision applies to the
  resolution of such deadlock instead of the court entering an order of judicial
  dissolution or an order directing the purchase of petitioner's interest under
  s. 605.0706." (2)(b) defines the term to include "a redemption or a purchase
  and sale of interests." Our Article 13 buy-sell IS such a provision, but it
  does not say so or cite s. 605.0702 — adding that, and making the timing
  explicit, would let the client point at the statute. **Drafting opportunity.**
- **s. 605.0702(1)(b)5** — deadlock is a ground for judicial dissolution only
  where the members cannot break it AND irreparable injury is threatened or
  suffered.

## Findings — ss. 605.0705-605.1108

**Dissolution deadlines that actually matter**
- **s. 605.0714(1)(a)** — the department may administratively dissolve a company
  that does not "deliver its annual report to the department by 5:00 p.m.
  Eastern Time on the third Friday in September of each year."
- **s. 605.0714(2)** — "Administrative dissolution ... for failure to file an
  annual report must occur on the fourth Friday in September of each year."
- **s. 605.0714(1)(c)** — also a ground: failure to "appoint and maintain a
  registered agent as required under s. 605.0113." Connects to the open question
  above about PS 2.
- **s. 605.0715(4)(a)** — reinstatement "relates back to and takes effect as of
  the effective date of the administrative dissolution."
- **s. 605.0715(5)** — the dissolved company's name is unavailable to others for
  1 year. s. 605.0717(2) — 120 days after dissolution or termination otherwise.
- **s. 605.0717(1)(d)** — dissolution does not terminate the registered agent's
  authority.

**Winding up**
- **s. 605.0710(2)** — after creditors, surplus goes FIRST to each person owning
  a transferable interest reflecting contributions made and not previously
  returned, in the amount of the unreturned contributions; THEN to members in
  the proportions in which they shared distributions. s. 605.0105(3)(j) makes
  only s. 605.0709(1), (2)(a) and (5) non-variable — s. 605.0710 IS variable, so
  our strictly-pro-rata liquidation override is permitted. It is also necessary
  on the S corporation form: the statutory default creates a return-of-capital
  preference that would look like a second class of stock.
- **s. 605.0711(11), 605.0712(3)(b)** — a member's liability for claims after
  dissolution is capped at the lesser of a pro rata share or what was
  distributed; s. 605.0712 gives a 4-year bar after filed or published notice.

**Appraisal rights — a gap in our masters**
- **s. 605.1006(1)** — a member has appraisal rights on a merger, conversion,
  interest exchange, **a sale of substantially all the assets** where the member
  could vote on it, and certain amendments to the organic rules.
- **s. 605.1006(2)** — the company "may modify, restrict, or eliminate the
  appraisal rights ... in its organic rules if the provision ... is authorized by
  each member whose appraisal rights are being modified, restricted, or
  eliminated."
- Our masters let a Majority in Interest sell substantially all the assets of the
  company or of a series but say nothing about appraisal rights, so a dissenting
  member keeps them. **Decide whether to waive.**
- **s. 605.1061(5)** — "fair value" is determined immediately before the event,
  using customary valuation techniques, "**without discounting for lack of
  marketability or minority status**." Our s. 10.4 and s. 13.2 both use "fair
  value" — worth confirming that no-discount is intended.

**Deadlock**
- **s. 605.0702(2)** — if the operating agreement contains a "deadlock sale
  provision that has been initiated before the time that the court determines
  that the grounds for judicial dissolution exist," it "applies to the resolution
  of such deadlock instead of the court entering an order of judicial
  dissolution or an order directing the purchase of petitioner's interest under
  s. 605.0706." (2)(b) includes "a redemption or a purchase and sale of
  interests." s. 605.0706(2), (3), (5) each carve out an interest already
  acquired under such a provision. **Our Article 13 qualifies but never says so.**

**Foreign entities — relevant to the out-of-state FAQ**
- **s. 605.0905(3)** — "The ownership in this state of income-producing real
  property or tangible personal property ... constitutes transacting business in
  this state."
- **s. 605.0904(1)** — a foreign LLC transacting business here without a
  certificate of authority "may not maintain an action or proceeding in this
  state."
- **s. 605.0904(7)** — liability for all back fees plus "a civil penalty of at
  least $500 but not more than $1,000 for each year or part thereof."
- **s. 605.0902(3)** — the department may require each series or cell of a
  foreign series LLC to apply separately for a certificate of authority.

**Florida income tax**
- **s. 605.1103(1)** — an LLC classified as a partnership, or a disregarded
  single-member LLC, "is not an 'artificial entity' within the purview of
  s. 220.02 and is not subject to the tax imposed under chapter 220."
- **s. 605.1103(2)** — but if the company is "classified otherwise for federal
  income tax purposes," it "shall be classified identically to its classification
  for federal income tax purposes" for chapter 220. **An S election changes the
  Florida classification analysis** — a question for the client's CPA that our S
  corporation material does not currently raise.
- **s. 605.1103(3)** — disregarded entities "must be treated as separate legal
  entities for all non-income tax purposes."

**Derivative and direct actions**
- **s. 605.0801(2)** — a direct action requires pleading and proving an injury
  not solely derivative, or a separate statutory or contractual duty.
- **s. 605.0802** — a derivative action requires a demand and "a reasonable time,
  not to exceed 90 days," unless futile.
- **s. 605.0804(5)** — on a motion to enforce a special litigation committee's
  determination the committee bears the burden of proving disinterest,
  independence, good faith and reasonable care.

**Misc**
- **s. 605.1101** — construe to promote uniformity with the uniform act.
- **s. 605.1108(2)** — the chapter governs all LLCs on or after January 1, 2015.
