# Chapter 605 — reading notes

Verified against the statutory text on Online Sunshine (leg.state.fl.us), read
through the browser rather than a summarizing fetch tool. The whole chapter is
417,137 characters; the protected series provisions (ss. 605.2101–605.2802) are
75,474 of them.

**Why this file exists.** A summarizer cannot tell you that you are wrong,
because what matters is usually the clause it dropped. Findings recorded here
are quoted or closely paraphrased from text actually read, so they can be
checked against the statute rather than against my memory of it.

**Effective date.** Ch. 2025-162 added ss. 605.2101–605.2802, effective
July 1, 2026. s. 605.2802(2): a domestic LLC formed before July 1, 2026 could
not create or designate a protected series before that date.

## CORRECTION — s. 605.2107 (non-variable provisions)

I previously asserted that s. 605.2107 does not make s. 605.2303 non-variable.
That is wrong. s. 605.2107(1) provides "An operating agreement may not vary the
effect of," and lists at (m) **"Section 605.2303(1) or (2)."**

Full list: (a) this section; (b) 605.2103; (c) 605.2104(1); (d) 605.2104(2), to
provide a protected series a power beyond those the chapter provides an LLC;
(e) 605.2104(3) or (4); (f) 605.2105; (g) 605.2106; (h) 605.2108; (i) 605.2201,
except the manner of approving establishment; (j) 605.2202; (k) 605.2301;
(l) 605.2302; (m) 605.2303(1) or (2); (n) 605.2304(3) or (6); (o) 605.2401,
except to decrease or eliminate a limitation of liability; (p) 605.2402;
(q) 605.2403; (r) 605.2404; (s) 605.2501(1), (4), (5); (t) 605.2502, except to
designate a different person to manage winding up; (u) 605.2503;
(v) 605.2601–605.2608; (w) 605.2701–605.2704; (x) 605.2801–605.2802, except who
may sign and deliver records; (y) provisions on registered offices/agents and on
the department.

s. 605.2107(2): an operating agreement may not unreasonably restrict the duties
and rights conferred under s. 605.2305, but may impose reasonable restrictions
on availability and use of information and provide remedies including liquidated
damages.

**Effect on our masters: none.** New s. 3.6(c) rests on s. 605.2303(4) and
s. 14.1 on s. 605.2501(2) — neither is in the non-variable list.

## Company-owned series — statutory support

- **s. 605.2303(1)** — a protected-series transferable interest "must be owned
  initially by an associated member of the protected series or the series
  limited liability company."
- **s. 605.2303(2)** — "If a protected series of a series limited liability
  company has no associated members when established, the company owns the
  protected-series transferable interests in the protected series."
- **s. 605.2303(3)** — the company may also acquire an interest by transfer or
  as the operating agreement provides.
- **s. 605.2303(4)** — chapter and operating-agreement provisions applying to a
  protected-series transferee apply to the company as owner, except
  s. 605.2108(1)(c).
- **s. 605.2302(1)** — "Only a member of a series limited liability company may
  be an associated member of a protected series of the company." The company
  cannot be one.
- **s. 605.2304(2)** — "If a protected series has no associated members, the
  series limited liability company is the protected-series manager." Not in the
  non-variable list, so the operating agreement may designate otherwise.
- **s. 605.2401(2)(c)3** — the company is NOT liable for a protected series'
  debts solely by reason of "owning a protected-series transferable interest of
  the protected series." Answers the obvious objection to company ownership.
- **s. 605.2105(4)(d), (5)(b)** — same point in the governing-law section.
- **s. 605.2403(2)** — s. 605.0503's charging-order restrictions apply to a
  judgment creditor of "a series limited liability company, to the extent the
  company owns a protected-series transferable interest of a protected series."
- **s. 605.2501** — no dissolution event for lacking associated members.
  (1) dissolution of the company; (2) an event the operating agreement states;
  (3) consent of all associated members; (4)–(5) judicial; (6) automatic or
  involuntary dissolution of the company; (7) administrative dissolution.
- **Definitional tension worth knowing:** s. 605.2102(10) defines a
  protected-series transferable interest as the right "as initially owned by a
  person in the person's capacity as an associated member." s. 605.2303(2) and
  s. 605.2105(4)(d)/(5)(b) and s. 605.2403(2) nevertheless expressly contemplate
  company ownership, so the structure holds.

## Shields, formalities, burden of proof

- **s. 605.2401(2)** — a debt of the company is solely the company's; a debt of
  a protected series is solely that series'; neither is liable for the other's
  solely by reason of the relationships listed.
- **s. 605.2402(2)** — "The failure of a limited liability company or a
  protected series to observe formalities relating to the exercise of its powers
  or management of its activities and affairs is **not** a ground to disregard a
  limitation in s. 605.2401(1) but **may** be a ground to disregard a limitation
  in s. 605.2401(2)." Sloppy formalities cannot reach the members personally but
  can collapse the shields between company and series. NOT currently on the site.
- **s. 605.2402(1)** — piercing claims are governed by law and equity as if each
  protected series were a separately formed LLC.
- **s. 605.2404(4)** — "the party asserting that an asset is or was an
  associated asset ... has the burden of proof on the issue." The owner carries
  it. NOT currently on the site.
- **s. 605.2404(2)** — a judgment against the company may be enforced against a
  series' asset that was or is a NON-associated asset on the incurrence or
  enforcement date, and vice versa, and between series.
- **s. 605.2301(2)(a)** — records must let a disinterested, reasonable
  individual identify the asset, determine when and from whom acquired, and, if
  acquired from the company or another series, the consideration, payor, payee.
- **s. 605.2301(4)** — records may be organized by listing, category, type,
  quantity, or computational or allocative formula, "or in any other reasonable
  manner."
- **s. 605.2301(5)** — a protected series may not hold an associated asset in
  the name of the company or another series, and vice versa.
- **s. 605.2301(2)(b)** — a recorded deed or instrument in favor of a person who
  gives value without knowledge of a lack of authority "is conclusive of the
  authority of the person signing and constitutes a record that such interest in
  real property is an associated asset or liability."

## Administration

- **s. 605.2203(1)** — "The registered agent in this state for a series limited
  liability company is the registered agent in this state for each protected
  series of that company." One agent covers everything. Supports charging no
  separate registered agent designation fee.
- **s. 605.2203(4)–(5)** — ceasing to be agent for the company ends the agency
  for every series, and vice versa.
- **s. 605.2206(1)–(2)** — the annual report under s. 605.0212 must name each
  protected series; failure "prevents issuance of a certificate of status
  pertaining to the protected series, but does not otherwise affect the
  protected series." No per-series fee appears in the statute — that is a
  Department fee-schedule question.
- **s. 605.2202(2)** — the name must begin with the company's name and contain
  "protected series" or the abbreviation "P.S." or "PS." No dash is required by
  statute; the dash is how Sunbiz renders it.
- **s. 605.2201(1)–(2)** — unanimous member vote or consent to establish; the
  designation states the company name and the series name.
- **s. 605.2104(2)–(4)** — a series has the same powers and purposes as the
  company EXCEPT that it may not be a member of the company, may not establish a
  protected series, and ceases to exist when the company completes winding up.
- **s. 605.2602–605.2604** — a protected series may not convert, domesticate, or
  participate in an interest exchange, and may merge only through the single
  channel in s. 605.2604 (every other party an LLC; surviving company not
  created in the merger).
- **s. 605.2108(1)** — the deeming rule applies in the application of
  ss. 605.2106, 605.2304(3) and (6), 605.2501(4)(a), 605.2502(1), and
  605.2503(2). (Our old statute page labeled 605.2503(2) a "claims process"; it
  is reinstatement. Section since removed.)

## General act — ss. 605.0101–605.0108 (read)

- **s. 605.0102(37) "Majority-in-interest"** is defined by PROFITS: members
  holding "more than 50 percent of the then-current percentage or other interest
  in the profits of the limited liability company owned by all of its members."
  Our masters define "Majority in Interest" by Percentage Interests. The two
  coincide in our forms because allocations and distributions are strictly pro
  rata to Percentage Interests, but the defined terms are not worded the same.
- **s. 605.0102(45)** — an operating agreement "may be oral, implied, in a
  record, or in any combination thereof." Our s. 15.1 says oral, implied, and
  course-of-dealing amendments are of no effect; s. 605.0105(1)(d) lets the
  agreement govern "the means and conditions for amending the operating
  agreement," so that restriction is permitted.
- **s. 605.0105(3)** — what an operating agreement may NOT do. Relevant limits:
  (c) registered agents and department filings; (e)–(f) may not eliminate the
  duty of loyalty or care, or the obligation of good faith and fair dealing;
  (g) may not exonerate bad faith, willful or intentional misconduct, or a
  knowing violation of law; (h) may not unreasonably restrict information rights
  under s. 605.0410; (i) may not vary the grounds for dissolution in
  s. 605.0702 — but expressly, **"A deadlock resolution mechanism does not vary
  the grounds for dissolution for the purposes of this paragraph"** (blesses our
  Article 13 deadlock/buy-sell); (k) may not unreasonably restrict a member's
  right to sue under ss. 605.0801–605.0806; (p) may not indemnify for bad faith,
  willful or intentional misconduct, improper personal benefit, or s. 605.0406
  liability.
- **s. 605.0105(4)(c)** — if not manifestly unreasonable, the agreement may
  alter or eliminate aspects of the duty of loyalty, identify categories of
  activity that do not violate it, alter the duty of care (but not authorize
  willful misconduct), and alter or eliminate any other fiduciary duty. Supports
  the Article 4 competition alternatives.
- **s. 605.0105(5)** — "manifestly unreasonable" is decided by the court as a
  matter of law, judged as of when the term entered the agreement.
- **s. 605.0106(5)** — a one-member operating agreement "is not unenforceable
  simply because there is only one person who is a party." Supports the
  single-member master.
- **s. 605.0106(6)** — an operating agreement is not subject to the statute of
  frauds except as provided in s. 605.0403(1), which is why our s. 6.1 requires
  a signed writing for a promised contribution.
- **s. 605.0107(4)** — where a filed record conflicts with the operating
  agreement, the agreement prevails as to members, dissociated members,
  transferees and managers; the record prevails as to others who reasonably rely
  on it.
- **s. 605.0103(4)** — a non-member is deemed to know of a limitation on
  authority to transfer real property under s. 605.0302(7), and to have notice
  of dissolution, termination, entity transactions, and a manager-managed
  declaration 90 days after the relevant filing takes effect. A limitation in
  the articles on authority to transfer real property is NOT notice to a
  non-member unless recorded in the real property records.
- **s. 605.0108(1)** — "A limited liability company is an entity distinct from
  its members."
